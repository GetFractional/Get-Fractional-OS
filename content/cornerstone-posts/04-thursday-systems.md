## LinkedIn Post Copy

Most creative projects don't fail because of bad creative.

They fail because nobody set the rules before work started.

Here are the 4 things that kill projects before delivery:

1. No brief structure. Creative gets built from a Slack thread and a "you know what we need" conversation. Then everyone is surprised when the output misses.

2. No revision policy. "Can you just tweak this?" becomes 11 rounds of feedback over 6 weeks. The project never ends.

3. No scope boundaries. "While you're at it, can you also..." turns a focused sprint into an open-ended engagement nobody budgeted for.

4. No testing plan. The creative ships, but nobody defined what to measure. So nobody knows what worked.

The fix isn't better talent. It's better structure.

Here's what I do differently:

- Scope agreement before work begins. What's in, what's out.
- 2 revision rounds. Consolidated feedback. No piecemeal notes.
- Clear milestones. Day 3 concept review. Day 10 draft review. Day 14 delivery.
- Testing plan baked into the kit. Not an afterthought.

Systems beat heroics. Every time.
