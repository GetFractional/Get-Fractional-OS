## Guide Content

### 7 Hooks That Beat Creative Fatigue
*Stop writing the same ad 10 different ways.*

---

#### The Problem

Creative fatigue isn't about running ads too long.

It's about running ads that all come from the same angle.

When every hook starts with "Tired of [problem]?" or "What if you could [benefit]?" — you're not testing creative. You're testing synonyms.

The fix: write hooks from different angles, not different words.

Here are 7 hook structures, each from a different angle. Use all 7 for your next product and you'll have more creative diversity than most brands run in a quarter.

---

#### Hook 1: The Pain Mirror
**Angle:** Empathy — reflect their exact frustration back at them.

> "You've tried 3 moisturizers this year. None of them lasted past noon."

**Why it works:** It skips the generic "Tired of dry skin?" and describes their specific experience. Specificity = credibility.

**Template:** "You've [specific action they've taken]. [Specific disappointing result]."

---

#### Hook 2: The Proof Lead
**Angle:** Evidence — open with a result, not a promise.

> "We tested 11 serums over 90 days. One outperformed the rest by 3x."

**Why it works:** Results first, product second. The reader's curiosity does the selling.

**Template:** "We [tested/compared/tracked] [number] [category]. [Specific finding]."

---

#### Hook 3: The Objection Flip
**Angle:** Preempt the reason they won't buy.

> "Yes, it's $89. Here's why customers who switched from $15 alternatives don't go back."

**Why it works:** Addresses price resistance head-on. People respect brands that don't avoid the hard question.

**Template:** "Yes, [acknowledged objection]. Here's why [proof that overcomes it]."

---

#### Hook 4: The Unexpected Insight
**Angle:** Pattern interrupt — say something they haven't heard.

> "Your skin doesn't need more moisture. It needs less of what's stripping it."

**Why it works:** Contradicts conventional wisdom. Forces a double-take. Earns the next 3 seconds.

**Template:** "Your [subject] doesn't need [expected solution]. It needs [unexpected reframe]."

---

#### Hook 5: The Specific Detail
**Angle:** Precision — replace vague claims with exact numbers.

> "The 3 ingredients dermatologists actually use on their own skin."

**Why it works:** "3 ingredients" is more interesting than "the best ingredients." Numbers create curiosity gaps.

**Template:** "The [specific number] [things] [authority figure] actually [does/uses/recommends]."

---

#### Hook 6: The Before/After Bridge
**Angle:** Transformation — show the gap between now and the outcome.

> "6 months ago, her morning routine was 45 minutes. Now it's 12."

**Why it works:** Concrete transformation. The reader maps their own "before" onto the hook.

**Template:** "[Time period] ago, [specific before state]. Now [specific after state]."

---

#### Hook 7: The Social Proof Snapshot
**Angle:** Other people's experience — let customers sell for you.

> "430 five-star reviews. The most common word? 'Finally.'"

**Why it works:** Volume + the unexpected detail. "Finally" implies a long search ending — that's more powerful than any feature list.

**Template:** "[Proof metric]. The most common [word/reaction/response]? '[Unexpected detail].'"

### Now What?

You have 7 hooks. But hooks are just the entry point.

- An ICP brief (so you know who you're hooking)
- An objection map (so you know what to address after the hook)
- 3–5 ad angles (so each hook leads somewhere different)
- A testing plan (so you know what worked)
